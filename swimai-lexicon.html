Why swim needs an AI lexicon

Competitive swimming already runs on data: splits, time standards, video, stroke rate, attendance, training cycles, and meet context. AI adds a new layer, but coaches need shared language before they can evaluate whether a tool is useful, safe, and valid.

Race intelligence

  • Separate meaningful race patterns from normal variation.
  • Ask whether predicted drops are ranges, not promises.
  • Connect split gaps to coach-actionable training priorities.

Video and stroke AI

  • Understand pose estimation, occlusion, frame rate, and camera angle bias.
  • Question whether labels were created by qualified reviewers.
  • Resist precision that exceeds video quality.

Athlete protection

  • Use stricter safeguards for minor athlete data.
  • Avoid permanent labels about potential, motivation, or risk.
  • Keep coaches responsible for context and final decisions.

Vendor vetting checklist

Use these questions before a club adopts a race-analysis, video-analysis, wearable, recruiting, or athlete-monitoring AI tool.

DomainQuestion to askEvidence to request
Population fitWas the system tested on swimmers like ours?Results by age, sex, stroke, event, course, level, and disability status where relevant.
Video conditionsWhat camera angles, frame rates, lighting, pool types, and underwater conditions were tested?Error rates by condition and examples of failure cases.
Measurement accuracyHow close are automated splits, turns, or stroke metrics to a trusted reference?Comparison with official timing, high-quality manual coding, or expert review.
Prediction claimsDoes the tool give a range or a single exact future time?Calibration data, prediction intervals, and past prospective validation.
Coach overrideCan coaches edit, reject, or annotate AI recommendations?Audit trail showing AI output, coach decision, and reason for override.
Minor dataWhat data are collected from minors and how is consent handled?Privacy terms, parent or guardian workflow, retention policy, access controls, and deletion process.
Data reuseCan athlete data be used to train the vendor's models?Contract language for opt-in, opt-out, de-identification, sale, sharing, and deletion.
Safety boundariesWhat recommendations are blocked?Guardrails against medical advice, unsafe training jumps, psychological labels, or direct unsupervised messaging to minors.
TransparencyCan the tool explain why it made a recommendation?Feature importance, supporting race segments, video frames, or rules used for the conclusion.
SecurityHow are video, wellness, contact, and performance data protected?Encryption, access logs, incident response, role-based permissions, and data processing agreements.
GovernanceWho owns review, approval, and monitoring after adoption?Club policy naming responsible coaches, administrators, and review cadence.
Marketing claimsWhich claims are evidence-based and which are promotional?Peer-reviewed studies, independent validation, or clear statement that evidence is preliminary.
Recommended use rule: AI can summarize patterns and raise questions. It should not independently determine training load, medical status, athlete potential, team placement, recruiting value, or communication with minors. The coach remains responsible for judgment, context, safety, and follow-through.
